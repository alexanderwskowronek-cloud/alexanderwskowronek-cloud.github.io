501 darts rules: double out and bust explained

Last updated: November 2026

In 501, each player starts on 501 and subtracts the score of three darts per visit. The winner is the first to reach exactly zero with the last dart in a double or the bullseye. Scoring more than you have left, leaving 1, or reaching zero without a double is a bust, and that visit does not count.

How does a leg of 501 work?

  1. Each player starts with 501 points. In 301 or 701 the starting score is lower or higher accordingly.
  2. Each visit is three darts. The score of the visit is subtracted from what you have left.
  3. The start is “straight in”: any dart inside the outer double wire scores straight away (WDF rule 2.01).
  4. The finish is a “double out”: the last dart must bring the score to exactly zero and land in a double or the bullseye (WDF rule 2.02).
  5. The first player to reach zero wins the leg. A dart thrown after finishing does not count (WDF rule 2.09).

What does double out mean?

Double out means you must hit the double that equals half of your remaining score. With 40 left that is D20, with 32 left D16. The bullseye (50) counts as double 25, so it can also finish a leg. An odd score such as 41 cannot be finished directly: you first set up a double with a single, for example S9 and then D16.

What is a bust?

Under WDF rule 2.05 a visit is bust and does not count if it

  • scores more points than you have left,
  • reaches exactly zero without the last dart being a double, or
  • leaves a score of 1, because 1 can no longer be finished on a double.

Your score goes back to what it was before that visit, and it is the next player's turn.

Who throws first?

In tournaments a bull throw decides the order: each player throws one dart at the bull, and the closer one starts (WDF rule 12.01). The winner of the bull throw starts the first leg and every odd-numbered leg, so the throw alternates from leg to leg (WDF rule 12.02).

What are legs and sets?

A leg is a single game from 501 down to zero. A match is usually played as “best of”, for example best of 5 legs: the first player to win three legs wins the match. In bigger events, legs are grouped into sets, and the player who wins the majority of sets wins the match (WDF rules 2.10 and 2.11).

What is the fewest number of darts to finish 501?

Nine. With eight darts the highest possible finish is 470: seven treble 20s (420) and the bull (50). A nine-darter is, for example, six treble 20s (360) and then T20 · T19 · D12 for the remaining 141.

Are there other formats?

Yes. The WDF rules set out straight start and double finish for tournament play, and an event's rules fix the starting score, such as 501, 701 or 1001 (WDF rule 3.01). According to PDC Europe, some PDC tournaments (currently only the World Grand Prix) are played “double in, double out”, where a leg must also be started with a double. In casual games, players sometimes agree on other formats, such as a single-out finish. Agree on the format before you start.
