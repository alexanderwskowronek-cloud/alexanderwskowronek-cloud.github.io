Wet iPhone: what not to do

Last updated: November 2026

Don't put a wet iPhone in rice, don't dry it with a hair dryer or other heat source or with compressed air, and don't push cotton swabs into the openings. That is Apple's own advice. Wipe it off, tap it out gently and leave it in a dry place with some airflow. Charge it by cable only once it is dry.

Should I put a wet iPhone in rice?

No. Apple explicitly advises against it, because small particles of rice could damage the iPhone. A dry place with some airflow is better.

Can I use a hair dryer?

No. Apple says not to dry the iPhone using an external heat source. That includes hair dryers, radiators and ovens.

Can I use compressed air?

No. Apple says not to dry the iPhone with compressed air and not to use it to clean dust off the phone either. A fan blowing cool air into the connector is a different thing: Apple says that might help the drying.

Can I use a cotton swab in the openings?

No. Don't insert a foreign object such as a cotton swab or a paper towel into the connector. For the speaker opening, Apple suggests at most a small, soft-bristled brush that is clean and dry.

Can I charge a wet iPhone?

Not by cable until it is dry. Apple recommends allowing at least 5 hours before charging with a Lightning or USB-C cable or connecting an accessory. If the iPhone shows a liquid-detection alert, unplug the cable and try again after at least 30 minutes. It can take up to 24 hours to fully dry. In an emergency, iPhone offers an option to override the alert, but Apple warns that charging while the connector is wet can corrode the pins and cause permanent damage.

What should I do instead?

  1. Wipe it off with a soft, lint-free cloth.
  2. Tap it gently against your hand with the connector facing down.
  3. Leave it in a dry area with some airflow.
  4. If the sound is muffled: place it speaker side down on a cloth and help with a low tone. The steps are in the guide How to get water out of your iPhone speaker.

Isn't my iPhone waterproof?

No. iPhone 7 and later are splash, water and dust resistant, rated IP67 or IP68 under IEC standard 60529 depending on the model. Apple states the tested maximum depth per model, from 1 meter up to 6 meters, for up to 30 minutes. This resistance is not a permanent condition and might decrease with normal wear. Apple also advises against swimming or showering with the iPhone. Liquid damage is not covered under Apple's warranty.

Sources

  • Apple Support: If you see a liquid-detection alert on your iPhone: no external heat source or compressed air, no cotton swab or paper towel in the connector, no rice, at least 30 minutes after an alert, up to 24 hours, emergency override, corrosion of the pins
  • Apple Support: About splash, water, and dust resistance of iPhone 7 and later: wipe, tap, airflow, a fan blowing cool air might help, at least 5 hours before charging by cable, IP67/IP68 under IEC 60529 with depths of 1 to 6 meters for up to 30 minutes, resistance not permanent, avoid swimming and showering, no compressed air for dust, warranty
  • Apple Support: If you hear no sound or distorted sound from your iPhone, iPad, or iPod touch speaker: cleaning openings with a small, soft-bristled, clean and dry brush
