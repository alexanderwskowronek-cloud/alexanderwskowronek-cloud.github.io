Klarton FAQ

Klarton is an iPhone app by an independent developer that plays a low tone to push water out of the speaker grille and measures how much of the sound gets through before and after.

How does Klarton work?

Klarton plays a low tone whose vibration pushes the droplets out of the speaker grille. One tap starts a run. You hold the phone at a slight angle with the speaker pointing down.

How long does a run take?

Fifteen seconds.

What does Klarton measure?

Klarton plays a series of test tones and listens through the microphone to how loudly they actually arrive. Measure once before a run and once after, and the comparison shows whether more of the tone gets through. The app also checks whether one of the two speakers sounds muffled.

Why not just use 165 hertz?

The often quoted 165 hertz is only an average across many device generations. Klarton looks for the frequency at which your particular device resonates most strongly. If that frequency clearly stands out, the water mode picks it up by itself.

What does Klarton need the microphone for?

Only for measuring. What arrives there stays in memory, is never stored and never leaves the device. Without a measurement the microphone is not switched on at all.

Which modes are there besides water?

A mode against dust in the grille, a gentle sweep from 60 to 400 hertz, and a manual setting anywhere from 60 hertz to 20 kilohertz.

What is the self-test?

The self-test asks you how the speaker sounds before and afterwards and puts the two side by side. There is no part inside a phone that could measure water in the speaker chamber, so Klarton does not invent a number for it.

Does Klarton help with water inside the phone?

No. Klarton helps with water in the speaker openings. Water inside the device is a case for a service appointment, not for a tone. How much one run achieves depends on the device and on the amount of water.

Does Klarton need an account or an internet connection?

No. You don't need an account or a sign-up. The only time Klarton goes online is to ask the App Store whether a newer version exists, without any identifier.
